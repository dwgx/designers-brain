## 一、书信（1916–1929，致 Anita Pollitzer / Stieglitz / Anderson / Luhan）

### 1. 致 Stieglitz（1916-10-26，得《浮士德》后）
> Yes there will be time to read it — When I want to I make time and I don't think I ever wanted to read anything so much before —

- 来源：欧姬芙博物馆博客「It's a Sketch!」（Tori Duggan, 2016-04-26；引 1916-10-26 信）；书为 Stieglitz 1916 年赠，题献「Faust / quieted me in such despairing / moments – always – …」。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：会的，会有时间读它——我想读的时候就会腾出时间；我不记得还有什么东西让我这么想读。

### 2. 致 Anita Pollitzer（纽约，1916-02）
> Anita, I have just come to the comforting conclusion that I'll have to paint acres and acres of water color landscapes before I will look for even a passably fair one. … It's like feeling around in the dark — thought I knew what I was going to try to do but I find I don't — and guess I'll only find out by slaving away at it. … It is a very special sort of sick feeling — and I like it.

- 来源：《The Complete Correspondence of Georgia O'Keeffe & Anita Pollitzer》（Touchstone, 1990），p. 145。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：安妮塔，我刚得出一个自我安慰的结论：我得画上成亩成亩的水彩风景，才可能找到哪怕一张勉强过得去的。……像在黑暗里摸索——以为知道自己要干什么，却发现并不知道——大概只能靠苦干找出来。……那是一种很特别的难受劲儿——可我喜欢。

### 3. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Canyon, TX，1916-09-11）
> Today I walked into the sunset — to mail some letters — … The Eastern sky was all grey blue — bunches of clouds — different kinds of clouds — sticking around everywhere and the whole thing — lit up — first in one place — then in another with flashes of lightning — sometimes just sheet lightning — and some times sheet lightning with a sharp bright zigzag flashing across it. I walked out past the last house — past the last locust tree — and sat on the fence for a long time — looking — just looking at — the lightning — you see there was nothing but sky and flat prairie land — land that seems more like the ocean than anything else I know.

- 来源：同 #2，pp. 183-184。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：今天我朝着日落走去——去寄几封信——……东边天空全是灰蓝色——一团团的云——各种各样的云——到处挂着，整个天——亮起来——先这儿、后那儿，闪着电——有时只是片状闪电——有时片状闪电里又劈出一道锐亮的之字形。我走过最后一户人家——走过最后一棵洋槐——在栅栏上坐了很久——看——就只是看着——那闪电——你知道，那儿除了天空和扁平的草原什么都没有——那地看起来比我知道的任何东西都更像海洋。

### 4. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Canyon, TX，1916-09-14）
> Last night I couldn't sleep till after four in the morning – I had been out to the canyon all afternoon – till late at night – wonderful color – I wish I could tell you how big – and with the night the colors deeper and darker – … Then the moon rose right up out of the ground after we got out on the plains again – battered a little where he bumped his head but enormous – There was no wind – it was just big and still – so very big and still.

- 来源：同 #2，pp. 186-187。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：昨晚我凌晨四点后才睡着——我整个下午都在峡谷里——直到深夜——色彩妙极了——我多希望能告诉你它有多大——入夜颜色更深更暗——……后来我们回到平原上，月亮就从地里直接升起来——大概磕到了头，有点瘪——可大得不得了。没有风——它只是又大又静——那么大，那么静。

### 5. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Canyon, TX，1916-09）
> Anita – I am so glad I'm out here – I can't tell you how much I like it. I like the plains – and I like the work [her painting] – everything is so ridiculously new – and there is something about it that just makes you glad you're living here – You understand – there is nothing here – so maybe there is something wrong with me that I am liking it so much.

- 来源：同 #2，p. 187。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：安妮塔——我真高兴我在这儿——我说不出我有多喜欢这里。我喜欢这平原——也喜欢这工作［指她的画］——一切都新得荒唐——这里有种东西，就是让你高兴自己活在这儿——你懂——这里什么都没有——所以也许是我哪里不对劲，才会这么喜欢它。

### 6. 致 Sherwood Anderson（1923-08-01）
> This morning I saw an envelope on the table Stieglitz addressed to you — I've wanted so often to write you — two things in particular to tell you — but I do not write — I do not write to anyone — maybe I do not like telling myself to people — and writing means that. … I think of you often because the few times you came to us were fine — like fine days in the mountains — fine to remember — clear sparkling and lots of air — fine air.

- 来源：《Georgia O'Keeffe: Art and Letters》（NGA, 1987）；经 The Marginalian 转录（「Georgia O'Keeffe on What It Means to Be an Artist」）。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：今早我在桌上看到一只 Stieglitz 写给你的信封——我常常想给你写信——尤其有两件事想告诉你——可我没有写——我不给任何人写信——也许我不喜欢把自己讲给别人听——而写信就意味着这个。……我常想起你，因为你来的那几次都很好——像山里晴朗的日子——值得记着——清亮、闪光、空气很多——很好的空气。

### 7. 致 Sherwood Anderson（1923-09）
> I feel that a real living form is the result of the individual's effort to create the living thing out of the adventure of his spirit into the unknown — where it has experienced something — felt something — it has not understood — and from that experience comes the desire to make the unknown — known. … if you stop to think of the form — as form you are lost —

- 来源：同 #6（《Art and Letters》1987；Marginalian 转录）。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：我觉得，一个真正活的形式，是个人努力的产物——是他的精神闯入未知的一次冒险，在其中经验到某种东西、感觉到某种东西——而尚未理解——由这经验，才生出把未知变成已知的渴望。……如果你停下来把形式当作形式去想——你就迷失了——

### 8. 致 Sherwood Anderson（1923-09，同信续）
> What others have called form has nothing to do with our form — I want to create my own and I can't do anything else — if I stop to think of what others — authorities or the public — or anyone — would say of my form I'd not be able to do anything. … I can never show what I am working on without being stopped — whether it is liked or disliked I am affected in the same way — sort of paralyzed — .

- 来源：同 #6。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：别人所谓的「形式」与我们的形式毫无关系——我要创造我自己的，别的我做不来——如果我停下来想别人——权威或公众——或任何人——会怎么说我的形式，我就什么都做不成了。……我一旦把正在做的东西拿给人看，就一定会被卡住——不管是被喜欢还是被讨厌，对我的影响都一样——像瘫痪了一样——

### 9. 致 Mabel Dodge Luhan（纽约，1925）
> I thought you could write something about me that men can't – What I want written – I do not know – … but a woman who has lived many things and who sees lines and colors as an expression of living – might say something that a man can't – I feel there is something unexplored about woman that only a woman can explore – Men have done all they can do about it.

- 来源：《Voicing our Visions: Writings by Women Artists》（Witzling 编, 1991），p. 224。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：我想你能写一点男人写不出的关于我的东西——具体想被写成什么，我不知道——……但一个经历了许多事的女人、一个把线条和颜色看作生命之表达的女人——也许能说出男人说不出的东西——我觉得女人身上有种未被勘探的东西，只有女人能勘探——男人在这件事上已经做尽了他们能做的。

### 10. 致 Ettie Stettheimer（新墨西哥→纽约列车上，1929-08-24）
> I am on the train going back to Stieglitz – and in a hurry to get there – I have had four months west and it seems to be all that I needed – It has been like the wind and the sun – there doesn't seem to have been a crack of the waking day or night that wasn't full – I haven't gained an ounce in weight but I feel so alive that I am apt to crack at any moment…

- 来源：同 #9，p. 226。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：我在回 Stieglitz 身边的火车上——急着到那儿——我在西部待了四个月，似乎这正是我需要的一切——像风像太阳——醒着的白天黑夜里，没有一道缝不是满的——我体重一两没涨，可我活得快要裂开了……

## 二、书信与声明（1930–1956）

### 11. 致 William M. Milliken（纽约，1930-11-01）
> I know I cannot paint a flower. I can not paint the sun on the desert on a bright summer morning but maybe in terms of paint color I can convey to you my experience of the flower or the experience that makes the flower of significance to me at that particular time. … I do know that the flower is painted large to convey to you my experience of the flower – and what is my experience of the flower if it is not color.

- 来源：同 #9，p. 227（两段合并引录）。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：我知道我画不了花。我也画不了明亮夏日早晨沙漠上的太阳；但也许用颜料和颜色，我能把我对花的体验——或那个让花在特定时刻对我变得重要的体验——传达给你。……我确知的是：这朵花被画得很大，是为了向你传达我对花的体验——而我对花的体验，如果不是颜色，又是什么。

### 12. 致 William Howard Schubart（Abiquiú，1950-08-04）
> It seems odd to think of you at Lake George tonight – I can smell the outdoors – and hear it – and see the stars – … There was no light little house – there were no people – there was only the night – I will never go back again – maybe to stand just for a moment where I put the little bit that was left of Alfred [Stieglitz] after he was cremated – but I think not even for that. I put him where he would hear the lake. – That is finished.

- 来源：同 #9，pp. 228-229。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：今晚想到你在乔治湖，感觉奇怪——我能闻到户外的气味——听见它——看见星星——……没有亮着灯的小屋——没有人——只有夜——我再也不会回去了——也许只站上一小会儿，在我安放阿尔弗雷德［Stieglitz］火化后留下那一点点的地方——但我想连这个也不会了。我把他放在了能听见湖的地方。——那已经结束了。

### 13. 致 William Howard Schubart（同信，谈创作与自我评判）
> About my work Howard – I always have two opinions – one is my way of seeing it for myself – and for myself I am never satisfied – never really – I almost always fail – always I think – now next time I can do it – Maybe that is part of what keeps one working – I can also look at myself – by that I mean my work from the point of view of the looking public – and that is the way I look at it when I think of showing. I have always first had a show for myself – and made up my mind – then after that it doesn't matter to me very much what anyone else say – good or bad.

- 来源：同 #9，p. 228。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：说我的作品，霍华德——我总有两种看法——一种是我自己看它的方式——对自己，我从不满意——从来没有真正满意过——我几乎总是失败——总想——下一次我能做到——也许这正是让人继续工作的原因之一——我也可以从「看画的公众」的角度看自己——也就是我的作品——想着展览时我就是这么看的。我从来先是给自己办一场展览——打定主意——那之后，别人说什么，好或坏，对我就没多大关系了。

### 14. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Abiquiú，1955-05-31）
> My spring has been much better than every travelling springs of the last two years — I have been working — or trying to work my garden into a kind of permanent shape. … At the moment I have three rose bushes so full of red and yellow roses that they look on fire — they are really astonishing …

- 来源：《Complete Correspondence》（Touchstone, 1990），p. 298。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：我这个春天比前两年到处跑的春天好得多——我一直在干活——或者说，一直在把花园整理成一种固定的样子。……眼下我有三丛玫瑰，开满了红与黄，看起来像着了火——真叫人吃惊……

### 15. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Abiquiú，1956-01-17，论「来源」）
> Dear Anita, don't forget Mary Cassatt [as one of her inspirations] — and I am not sure that your new paragraph will hold water — We [artists] probably all derive from something — with some it is more obvious than with others — so much so that we can not escape a language of line that has been growing in meaning since the beginning of lines.

- 来源：同 #14，p. 305。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：亲爱的安妮塔，别忘了玛丽·卡萨特［作为她的启发者之一］——还有，我不确定你新写的那段站得住脚——我们［艺术家］大概都从什么东西里来——只是有些人更明显——明显到我们逃不开一种线条的语言，它从线条诞生之初就在生长意义。

### 16. 致 Anita Pollitzer（Abiquiú，1968-02-28，拒绝「神话」）
> Dear Anita, I read your manuscript some time ago and it has lain on my table — ..You have written your dream picture of me — and I am not that way at all. … You write of the legends others have made up about me — but when I read your manuscript, it seems as much a myth as all the others. I really believe that to call this my biography when it has so little to do with me is impossible — and I cannot have my name exploited to further it.

- 来源：同 #14，p. 320。
- 类型：[self-written]（书信）
- 中文意译：亲爱的安妮塔，我前些时候读了你的稿子，它一直摊在我桌上——……你写的是你梦里的那个我——我根本不是那样。……你写的是别人编造出来的关于我的传说——可我读你的稿子时，它和所有别的传说一样像个神话。我真的觉得，把它叫作我的传记——而它与我关系如此之少——是不可能的；我不能让我的名字被利用来促成它。

### 17. 致 Anita Pollitzer（笔记，1968-02 之后）
> I do not like the idea of happiness — it is too momentary — I would say that I was always busy and interested in something — interest has more meaning to me than the idea of happiness.

- 来源：同 #14，p. 324。
- 类型：[self-written]（笔记/书信附件）
- 中文意译：我不喜欢「幸福」这个说法——它太短暂了——我会说，我总是忙着、总对什么东西感兴趣——「兴趣」对我比「幸福」更有意义。

## 三、展览声明与自传（1922–1976）

### 18. 纽约《太阳报》访谈（1922-12-05，「I Can't Sing, So I Paint!」）
> "Nothing is less real than realism," said Georgia O'Keeffe. "Details are confusing. It is only by selection, by elimination, by emphasis, that we get at the real meaning of things." … "Singing has always seemed to me the most perfect means of expression," she said. "It is so spontaneous. And after singing, I think the violin. Since I cannot sing I paint."

- 来源：New York Sun（1922-12-05），p. 22；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（报刊访谈，直接引语）
- 中文意译：「没有什么比写实更不真实，」欧姬芙说。「细节令人困惑。只有通过选择、剔除、强调，我们才能抵达事物的真正意义。」……「歌唱在我看来一直是最完美的表达方式，」她说。「它那么自然。歌唱之后，我想是小提琴。既然我不能唱，我就画。」

### 19. Anderson Galleries 展览场刊声明（1923-01）
> I grew up pretty much as everybody else grows up and one day seven years ago found myself saying to myself — I can't live where I want to — I can't go where I want to — I can't do what I want to — I can't even say what I want to —. School and things that painters have taught me even keep me from painting as I want to. I decided I was a very stupid fool not to at least paint as I wanted to … —I found that I could say things with color and shapes that I couldn't say in any other way — things that I had no words for. … Some of the first drawings done to please myself I sent to a girl friend requesting her not to show them to anyone. She took them to 291 and showed them to Alfred Stieglitz and he insisted on showing them to others. He is responsible for the present exhibition.

- 来源：场刊《Alfred Stieglitz Presents One Hundred Pictures: Oils, Water-colors, Pastels, Drawings by Georgia O'Keeffe, American》（The Anderson Galleries, New York, 1923-01-29 起）；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录（wikiquote 另注 1926 年场刊前言沿用同文）。
- 类型：[self-written]（展览声明）
- 中文意译：我长大的方式和别人差不多；七年前的某一天，我发现自己对自己说——我不能住在我想住的地方——不能去我想去的地方——不能做我想做的事——连我想说的话都不能说。学校和画家们教我的东西，甚至妨碍我按自己的意愿作画。我认定自己是个大傻瓜：至少该按自己想画的样子去画……——我发现我能用颜色和形状说出我无法用别的方式说出的东西——我找不到词的东西。……一些最初画来取悦自己的画，我寄给一位女朋友，请她不要给任何人看。她带去了 291，给阿尔弗雷德·Stieglitz 看，他坚持要展示给别人。这次展览要归功于他。

### 20. Anderson Galleries 场刊声明（1924-03，回应批评）
> I am asked to write a statement for my catalogue. … My reason for the present exhibition is that I feel that my work of this year may clarify some of the issues written of by the critics and spoken about…

- 来源：场刊《Alfred Stieglitz Presents Fifty-One Recent Pictures…》（Anderson Galleries, 1924-03）；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-written]（展览声明）
- 中文意译：有人请我为图录写一份声明。……我办这次展览的理由是：我觉得我今年的作品也许能澄清一些批评家写到过、谈论过的问题……（背景：1923–24 年间批评界大量以弗洛伊德式性解读其作品；1924 年声明意在「劝阻」——见档案 §11。）

### 21. 「About Myself」（An American Place 场刊，1939-01）
> A flower is relatively small. Everyone has many associations with a flower — the idea of flowers. You put out your hand to touch the flower — lean forward to smell it — maybe touch it with your lips almost without thinking — or give it to someone to please them. Still — in a way — nobody sees a flower — really — it is so small — we haven't time — and to see takes time like to have a friend takes time. If I could paint the flower exactly as I see it no one would see what I see because I would paint it small like the flower is small. So I said to myself — I'll paint what I see — what the flower is to me but I'll paint it big and they will be surprised into taking time to look at it — I will make even busy New Yorkers take time to see what I see of flowers. Well — I made you take time to look at what I saw and when you took time to really notice my flower you hung all your own associations with flowers on my flower and you write about my flower as if I think and see what you think and see of the flower — and I don't.

- 来源：《Georgia O'Keeffe: Exhibition of Oils and Pastels》场刊（An American Place, New York, 1939-01-22 起）；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录（NGA 学术条目脚注 [19] 同源）。
- 类型：[self-written]（展览声明）
- 中文意译：一朵花是相对小的。每个人对花都有许多联想——「花」这个观念。你伸手去碰它——俯身去闻——也许几乎不假思索地用嘴唇碰一下——或送给别人讨人欢心。可是——在某种意义上——没有人真正看见一朵花——真的——它太小了——我们没有时间——而「看」是需要时间的，就像交朋友需要时间。如果我把花画得和我看见的一模一样，谁也不会看见我所看见的，因为我会把它画得像花本身一样小。于是我对自已说——我要画我所看见的——画这朵花对我是怎样的，但我要把它画得很大，他们就会惊得停下来看——我要让忙碌的纽约人也花时间看看我所看见的花。结果——我让你们花时间看了我所看到的；而当你们真的注意到我的花时，你们把你们对花的所有联想都挂在我的花上，写我的花时好像我想的、看的就是你们想的、看的——而我不是。

### 22. 「About Painting Desert Bones」（An American Place 场刊，1944-01）
> I have picked flowers where I found them — Have picked up sea shells and rocks and pieces of wood where there were sea shells and rocks and pieces of wood that I liked. When I found the beautiful white bones on the desert I picked them up and took them home too I have used these things to say what is to me the wideness and wonder of the world as I live in it. … I was the sort of child that ate around the raisin on the cookie and ate around the hole in the doughnut saving either the raisin or the hole for last and best So probably — not having changed much — when I started painting the pelvis bones I was most interested in the holes in the bones — what I saw through them — particularly the blue from holding them up in the sun against the sky… They were most wonderful against the Blue — that Blue that will always be there as it is now after all man's destruction is finished.

- 来源：《Georgia O'Keeffe: Paintings — 1943》场刊（An American Place, New York, 1944-01-11 起）；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-written]（展览声明）
- 中文意译：我在哪里找到花就在哪里采——也捡贝壳、石头和木片，只要是我喜欢的。当我在沙漠里发现那些美丽的白骨，我也把它们捡起来带回家。我用这些东西说出：在我生活的世界里，它有多辽阔、多惊奇。……我是那种吃饼干先绕着葡萄干吃、吃甜甜圈先绕着洞吃的孩子——把葡萄干或洞留到最后、留作最好的。所以大概——我没怎么变——当我开始画骨盆时，最感兴趣的是骨头上的洞——透过它们看到的东西——尤其是把骨头举在太阳下、对着天空时看到的蓝……它们对着那蓝最美——那蓝永远会在那里，一如现在，在人类一切毁坏结束之后。

### 23. 夏威夷声明（An American Place 场刊，1940-02）
> If my painting is what I have to give back to the world for what the world gives to me, I may say that these paintings are what I have to give at present for what three months in Hawaii gave to me. … To formulate the new experience into something one has to say takes time. Maybe the new place enlarges one's world a little. Maybe one takes one's own world along and cannot see anything else.

- 来源：《Georgia O'Keeffe: Exhibition of Oils and Pastels》场刊（An American Place, 1940-02-03 起）；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-written]（展览声明）
- 中文意译：如果我的画是我对世界给我的东西的回赠，那我可以说：这些画是我此刻对夏威夷三个月给我的东西的回赠。……要把新经验形成某种非说不可的东西，需要时间。也许新地方把一个人的世界扩大了一点。也许人带着自己的世界同行，别的什么也看不见。

### 24. 《Some Memories of Drawings》（1976，Viking 自传同期出版物）
> It is surprising to me to see how many people separate the objective from the abstract. Objective painting is not good painting unless it is good in the abstract sense. A hill or tree cannot make a good painting just because it is a hill or a tree. It is lines and colours put together so that they say something. For me that is the very basis of painting. The abstraction is often the most definite form for the intangible thing in myself that I can only clarify in paint. … I found I could say things with color and shapes that I couldn't say any other way — things I had no words for.

- 来源：《Some Memories of Drawings》（University of New Mexico Press / Viking, 1976）及 1976 年 Viking《Georgia O'Keeffe》；经 wikiquote 与欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」双源转录（末句与 #19 同文重复出现）。
- 类型：[self-written]（出版物）
- 中文意译：看到那么多人把「客观」与「抽象」分开，我很惊讶。客观的绘画，除非在抽象意义上也是好的，否则不是好画。一座山或一棵树，不能因为它是一座山或一棵树就成了好画。它是被组合起来、能说出些什么的线条与颜色。对我来说，这就是绘画的根本。抽象，往往是我内心里那不可言说之物最确定的形态——只有在颜料里我才能把它弄清楚。……我发现我能用颜色和形状说出我无法用别的方式说出的东西——我找不到词的东西。

### 25. 《Some Memories of Drawings》——论「无限」
> The unexplainable thing in nature that makes me feel the world is big fat beyond my understanding — to understand maybe by trying to put it into form. To find the feeling of infinity on the horizon line or just over the next hill.

- 来源：同 #24（另见 Barbara Rose, "O'Keeffe's Trail", New York Review of Books, 1977-03-31 引同句）。
- 类型：[self-written]（出版物）
- 中文意译：自然中那无法解释的东西让我感到：世界又大又饱满，超出我的理解——也许通过试着把它放进形式里来理解。去在地平线上、或在下一座山冈上，找到「无限」的感觉。

## 四、报刊访谈与晚年（1962–1986）

### 26. Katharine Kuh 访谈（1962，论「为什么画大」）
> In the twenties, huge buildings seemed to be going up overnight in New York. At that time I saw a painting by Fantin-Latour, a still life of flowers I found very beautiful, but I realized were I to paint the flowers so small, no one would look at them because I was unknown. So I thought I'll make them look big like the huge buildings going up. People will be startled; they'll have to look at them—and they did.

- 来源：Katharine Kuh,《The Artist's Voice: Talks with Seventeen Artists》（New York, 1962），pp. 190-191；经 NGA Torchia 学术条目（脚注 [18]，转录自 Geldzahler 1965, pp. 131-132）逐字引录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈）
- 中文意译：二十年代，纽约仿佛一夜之间冒出许多巨大的建筑。那时我看到方丹-拉图尔的一幅画，一幅花卉静物，我觉得很美；但我意识到，如果我把花画得那么小，没人会看它们——因为我没有名气。于是我想：我要把它们画得像那些拔地而起的大楼一样大。人们会吓一跳；他们不得不看——而他们确实看了。

### 27. 《Atlantic》访谈（1965-04，论自由与并存）
> I'm always swinging from one thing to the other. I have always been very free in my approach. I see no reason why abstract and realistic art can't live side by side. The principles are the same. I like to be interested. And I paint what interests me. … "Many people objected to me in the beginning," she remembers, "because I didn't fit into tradition. In those days you had to be a follower."

- 来源：Ralph Looney, "Georgia O'Keeffe", The Atlantic 215, no. 4（1965-04），pp. 106-113；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈）
- 中文意译：我总在从一件事荡到另一件事。我的方法一向很自由。我看不出为什么抽象与写实的艺术不能并存。原则是一样的。我喜欢对什么感兴趣。我画让我感兴趣的东西。……「很多人一开始反对我，」她回忆道，「因为我不合传统。那时候你必须做个追随者。」

### 28. 《纽约时报》访谈（1970-10-18，论「性符号」）
> "When people read erotic symbols in my paintings they're really talking about their own affairs," she snaps. "I remember how upset I was once by a certain reference Marsden Hartley made to my work in a book. I almost wept. I thought I could never face the world again. But a friend told me, 'Never mind. He's really writing about himself.'"

- 来源：Grace Glueck, "It's Just What's In My Head", The New York Times（1970-10-18），sec. 2, p. 24；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈）
- 中文意译：「人们在我的画里读出情色符号时，其实是在谈他们自己的事，」她厉声说。「我记得有一次马斯登·哈特利在书里提到我的作品，我难受极了。我差点哭出来。我以为我再也没法面对这个世界。但一个朋友告诉我：『别在意。他其实写的是他自己。』」

### 29. 《纽约杂志》访谈（1974-02-11，再论「情色」）
> "Eroticism!" she exclaimed in cool disdain. "That's something people themselves put into the paintings. They've found things that never entered my mind. That doesn't mean they weren't there, but the things they said astonished me. It wouldn't occur to me. But Alfred talked that way and people took it from him."

- 来源：Dorothy Seiberling, "The Female View of Erotica", New York Magazine 7, no. 6（1974-02-11），pp. 54-58；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈）
- 中文意译：「情色！」她冷冷地、不屑地叫出声。「那是人们自己放进画里的东西。他们找到的东西从没进过我的脑子。这不意味着它们不在那里，但他们说的那些话让我吃惊。我不会那么想。可阿尔弗雷德那么说，人们就从他那儿接了过去。」

### 30. 晚年（1970–1986，三则）
> (a) "I was flying and saw them—the most extraordinary things. It looked as if you could walk right out of the plane. When I get started with a theme I have to keep on going, so I did four versions. I made the last one that size because—well, I went to a place where they had big pictures and I thought I could do a better one."（1970，同 #28 来源）
> (b) "I think that what I have been able to give that is important has been my painting," Miss O'Keeffe said. "This will be available for people to see on museum walls for many years."（1983-11-08，NYT）
> (c) "I hear from so many people who love my work," she told Kotz. "The other day I had the nicest gift. It was a letter from a woman who said I had changed her life. She enclosed a dried milkweed pod in her letter. And the nice thing was that she gave no return address. Her gift was for me not to feel I had to take time to answer."（1986，Washington Post 追忆）

- 来源：(a) 同 #28；(b) William E. Farrell & Warren Weaver, Jr., "Briefing: Switch — by Georgia O'Keeffe", NYT（1983-11-08），sec. A, p. 24；(c) Paul Richard, "Appreciation: The Enduring Light of Georgia O'Keeffe", Washington Post（1986-03-07）。均经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈/报刊）
- 中文意译：(a)「我在飞机上看到的——最非凡的东西。好像你能直接从飞机里走出去。我一进入一个主题就必须一直走下去，所以画了四个版本。最后一张画那么大，是因为——嗯，我去过一个挂大画的地方，我想我能画一张更好的。」(b)「我认为我能够给出的重要的东西就是我的画，」欧姬芙说。「它们会在博物馆的墙上供人观看许多年。」(c)「那么多喜欢我作品的人给我写信，」她告诉科茨。「前几天我收到最好的礼物：一位女士写信说她改变了她的人生。信里夹着一枚干枯的马利筋果荚。最妙的是她没留回信地址。她的礼物就是让我不必觉得非得花时间回信。」

## 五、同时代记录与后世接受（1931–2026）

### 31. 展览场刊与「版本」方法（1931-12-29）
> "As a matter of fact," she said, "bones and flowers run together in my mind when I think of the desert. The bones bleach such a beautiful white lying out in the sun. I have always admired them… Then I thought how well they went with the ridiculous artificial flowers that the women have all over the place. And so I have put them together — actually to express what I feel about the desert." … "Of course, I went at it with a very realistic approach. I always do seven or eight different versions of the same thing, until I evolve the final product, like this."

- 来源：Ishbel Ross, "Bones of Desert Blaze Art Trail of Miss O'Keeffe…", New York Herald Tribune（1931-12-29），p. 3；经欧姬芙博物馆「In Her Own Words」转录。
- 类型：[self-spoken]（访谈）
- 中文意译：「事实上，」她说，「想到沙漠时，骨头和花在我脑子里是走到一起的。骨头躺在太阳下，晒成那么美的白。我一直欣赏它们……后来我想，它们和女人们到处摆的那种荒唐的人造花配在一起多好。于是我把它们放到了一起——其实是为了表达我对沙漠的感受。」……「当然，我是用非常写实的方式进入的。同一件东西我总是做七、八个不同的版本，直到演化出最后的样子，像这张。」

### 32. 《纽约时报》1946 年回顾展记录（[contemporary record]）

- 来源：JSMA 艺术家资源页（"In 1946, MoMA devoted their first exhibition to a woman artist to O'Keeffe with 57 paintings organized chronologically."）；MoMA 档案。另见 MoMA 2023 展览《To See Takes Time》checklist（45 页）。
- 类型：[contemporary record]（馆史记录）
- 中文意译：（转录）1946 年 MoMA 为欧姬芙举办 57 件绘画的编年个展——该馆首次以女性艺术家为主角的个展。

### 33. 《纽约客》漫画（1929，接受史）

- 来源：NGA Torchia 学术条目（脚注 [7]：New Yorker, July 6, 1929）。

- 中文意译：（转录）「百合圣母」——早期公众接受把「花」等同于她本人，这一形象持续至今。

### 34. 批评史两极（1927/1996，[later reception]）

- 类型：[later reception]（研究/批评）
- 中文意译：(b)「……把自己从那一性别身份的文化含义中豁免出去的意志……一种『借用并动用关键男性能指』的反讽努力——这仍是她为调适自己的接受条件所做的最极端的努力。」
